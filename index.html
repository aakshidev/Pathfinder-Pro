Application Improvement Tips

Click any section to expand personalised tips:

  • 1Start with a specific, vivid moment — not a generic "I want to change the world" opener. Show, don't tell.
  • 2Reveal your personality: quirks, humour, genuine passion. Admissions committees read thousands — yours must sound like you.
  • 3Draft early (June/July for fall admission). Revise at least 4 times. Get feedback from a trusted mentor.
  • 4For "Why this College/University?" essays — mention specific faculty, labs, clubs, or courses. Generic praise backfires.
  • 5Keep your Statement of Purpose (SOP) achievement-driven with quantifiable outcomes wherever possible.
  • 1JEE/NEET: Solve 5–10 years of previous papers. Focus on NCERT thoroughly before moving to advanced material.
  • 2CUET: Domain subjects follow NCERT strictly. English section benefits from daily reading practice.
  • 3SAT/ACT: Khan Academy's free SAT prep is excellent. Practice under timed, real test conditions.
  • 4IELTS/TOEFL: Target 7.0+ IELTS or 100+ TOEFL. Practise all four skills daily for 3–4 months.
  • 5Identify weak topics using mock tests and spend 80% of prep time on those, not your strengths.
  • 6Time management in-hall matters as much as knowledge. Simulate exam conditions in every practice session.
  • 1Depth over breadth — 2–3 deeply committed activities > 10 surface-level ones. Show leadership and impact.
  • 2For tech applications: build 3–4 GitHub projects and a portfolio site. Open-source contributions stand out.
  • 3Participate in national/international Olympiads, hackathons, or science fairs — these signal genuine interest.
  • 4Volunteer work related to your intended field shows commitment beyond grades.
  • 5Research internships (even virtual ones) add immense weight to Science, Engineering, and Medical applications.
  • 1Choose recommenders who know you well — not just the most popular teacher or the one whose subject you topped.
  • 2Ask at least 3–4 months in advance. Provide them a "brag sheet" listing your achievements, goals, and stories.
  • 3A warm, story-driven letter beats a generic "This student is excellent" letter every single time.
  • 4For Indian college applications to abroad: a research supervisor's recommendation carries more weight than a school teacher's.
  • 1India: Apply for NSP, state scholarships, and institution-specific merit awards. Check AICTE & UGC portals.
  • 2Abroad: Submit FAFSA (USA) or Student Finance (UK) as early as possible — many grants are first-come, first-served.
  • 3Search Fastweb, Scholarships.com, and Buddy4Study for subject-specific scholarships.
  • 4Inlaks, Tata Trust, and Narotam Sekhsaria Foundation offer prestigious scholarships for Indian students abroad.
  • 5Don't neglect college-specific merit aid — many institutions auto-consider applicants above a GPA/rank threshold.
  • 1Research the institution thoroughly — recent faculty research, notable alumni, unique programs. Drop specific names.
  • 2Prepare a crisp 90-second self-introduction. Practice it out loud until it flows naturally.
  • 3For MBA/management GDs: structure your points (Point → Reason → Example). Listen actively before speaking.
  • 4Dress professionally for online interviews — your entire visible space (background, lighting) signals seriousness.
  • 5Have 2–3 thoughtful questions ready to ask the interviewer. It shows genuine interest.
